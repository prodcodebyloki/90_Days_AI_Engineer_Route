After carefully weighing the arguments presented by both debaters regarding the motion "AI will do more good than harm to society," it is clear that the pro-AI debater made a more compelling case overall.

The first argument highlighted the potential of AI to revolutionize healthcare, presenting concrete examples of how AI can lead to faster and more accurate diagnoses, ultimately saving lives. This argument is persuasive because it provides specific, positive impacts that AI is already having in a critical area of society—healthcare. The emphasis on ongoing advancements in AI technologies makes this argument particularly convincing.

The pro-AI debater also illustrated how AI enhances efficiency and productivity across various sectors, claiming that it can lead to job creation as economies adapt. While acknowledging the concern of job displacement, the emphasis on re-skilling and education presents a balanced response that demonstrates a potential for societal growth rather than decline.

Additionally, the argument for AI’s role in addressing climate change is crucial. The examples provided about AI optimizing energy usage and aiding sustainable agriculture depict AI as a vital component in tackling one of the most pressing issues of our time. This relevance significantly strengthens the pro-AI argument.

Moreover, the focus on inclusivity and accessibility through advanced technologies reflects a positive societal change that is essential in today’s diverse world. This perspective aligns AI as a tool empowering marginalized groups, thus promoting a more equitable society.

In contrast, while the arguments against AI do present valid concerns such as job displacement, ethical issues, privacy infringements, safety risks, and existential threats, many of these concerns are speculative, particularly regarding long-term risks and the potential for catastrophic failures. The negative impacts cited revolved mainly on theoretical outcomes and ethical dilemmas that, while important, may not yet reflect the current state and deployed benefits of AI technologies.

Furthermore, the broad claims regarding the existential threats posed by AI seem less immediate than the substantial benefits illustrated by the pro-AI argument. The claims of mass surveillance and loss of privacy, while valid, do not outweigh the concrete advantages posited by the affirmative side.

In conclusion, while there are significant concerns associated with AI, the affirmative arguments regarding its benefits—especially in healthcare, productivity, environmental sustainability, and inclusivity—are more convincing based on the evidence and specificity provided. Therefore, I affirm that AI will do more good than harm to society.
