Ladies and gentlemen, I stand before you today to argue against the motion that AI will do more good than harm to society. While the proponents of AI often focus on its potential benefits, a closer examination reveals undeniable risks and harms that cannot be overlooked.

Firstly, the rapid advancement of AI technologies exacerbates existing inequalities. Rather than providing equitable access to opportunities, AI can entrench systemic biases and discrimination. Algorithms trained on historical data often reflect and reinforce societal prejudices, leading to discriminatory outcomes in critical areas like hiring, lending, and criminal justice. As a result, marginalized communities are disproportionately affected, worsening societal divisions rather than healing them.

Secondly, the specter of job displacement poses a significant threat to workers across various industries. Automation facilitated by AI technologies does not simply create new jobs; it eliminates existing ones at an alarming rate. Many workers face a stark reality of unemployment without adequate pathways for retraining or transitioning to new roles. This disruption can lead to increased economic instability and social unrest, particularly if we fail to address the gaps in our education and workforce development systems.

Moreover, the centralization of power in AI development raises alarming ethical concerns. A small number of tech companies are at the helm of AI advancements, shaping society according to their interests rather than the public good. This concentration of power risks magnifying existing corporate control over our lives, leading to a surveillance-driven society where individuals are constantly monitored and manipulated. We must be wary of the implications this holds for personal privacy and autonomy.

Additionally, the issue of accountability in AI systems remains unresolved. When AI makes decisions that impact lives—whether in healthcare, law enforcement, or finance—who is responsible for the consequences? As autonomous systems operate with a degree of opacity, holding individuals or organizations accountable for harmful outcomes becomes exceedingly challenging. This lack of accountability can erode trust in institutions and create a culture of impunity.

Lastly, the existential threat posed by autonomous weaponry and advanced AI in military applications cannot be ignored. The potential for AI to be weaponized escalates the risks of conflicts and unintended consequences in warfare, as machines may make decisions lacking human empathy and judgment. This development poses a grave threat not only to our societies but also to global stability.

In conclusion, while AI does hold the capacity for advancement, the potential harms it brings—reinforcing inequality, displacing jobs, centralizing power, lacking accountability, and posing existential threats—far outweigh its good. Therefore, we must approach AI with caution, recognize the real dangers it presents, and prioritize a framework that safeguards society rather than propagates harm. Thank you.
